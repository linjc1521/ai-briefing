AnthropicMaths

The Claude-assisted Riemann proof shrinks from 17 million lines to 76,000

On 25 Nov, Anthropic's Luke Sernau published grh-lean, a rewrite of Levent Alpöge's Lean proof of the Riemann hypothesis (and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions), made "in collaboration with my colleagues at Anthropic, and Claude". It comes in a readable edition of about 300,000 lines and a compact one of about 76,000, proving the same five theorems.

MIT's Andrew Sutherland ran independent checks, using Claude Code to run them. Both editions compile, rely only on Lean's three standard axioms, and pass Lean's paranoid comparator. One run on the readable edition stopped without a verdict because it ran out of memory. The repo says plainly: "No mathematician has read it through, and no paper comes with it."

South KoreaAssessment

South Korea won't ban AI in high-school performance assessments

Korea's education ministry said on 26 Nov that AI "will not be banned" in high-school performance assessments from next year. Its approach is safe, educational use: AI can help, for example, to explore issues or gather material. But students can't use it to analyse causes and effects or to write the presentation. Students record how they used AI, and teachers assess what they observe in class. An "AI Use Guide" is due by the end of the year.

China starts AI-literacy training for every teacher

At a Beijing meeting on 27 Nov, the Ministry of Education relaunched the Teacher Development Center on the National Smart Education Platform with an AI-literacy section, and began training for all teachers.
