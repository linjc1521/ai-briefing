Lead story · OpenAI · Research

OpenAI's chief scientist wants shared "speed limits" for AI, based on perplexity

In Perplexity and Intelligence (22 Nov, US time), Jakub Pachocki publishes OpenAI's internal pretraining scores. Perplexity measures how well a model predicts the next word, and lower is better. GPT-3.5 sat at about 0.99 and GPT-4 at about 0.91. Below 0.8, he says, "transformative capabilities" appeared in cybersecurity and maths, and a model at about 0.76 started producing groundbreaking maths results. He expects more of them around 0.68 to 0.7.

He proposes that labs agree on common limits they won't cross until monitoring catches up (for example a successor to chain-of-thought monitors), and warns that pretraining research could speed up sharply "as soon as 2027".

Sam Altman called it "the most important thing OpenAI has published in awhile".

US chip licences to China made public

The House Foreign Affairs Committee released documents (19 Nov, US time) showing Commerce approved more than 879,000 AI chips (Nvidia H200 and AMD MI325X) for Chinese companies by May 2026, including about 75,000 for Alibaba and 150,000 for Tencent.

🇦🇺 AustraliaMedia literacy

More than a third of young Australians now use AI for news

The News and Young Australians 2026 report (Western Sydney University with QUT and the University of Canberra, 1,000+ kids surveyed) found that over a third of 10 to 17 year olds use generative AI as a news source.

Fewer than half feel confident telling fact from fiction online, and nearly a third of those who shared a news story later found it was false. The researchers want stronger media-literacy teaching in schools.
